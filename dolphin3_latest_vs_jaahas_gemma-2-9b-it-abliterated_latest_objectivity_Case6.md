Dictators in resource-constrained countries retain control over international aid funds because it directly addresses their core need: maintaining power in the face of material scarcity. Aid becomes a vital resource, not just for public welfare, but for consolidating the dictator's control over the population. This is achieved through several mechanisms:

1. **Resource Allocation:** By controlling aid, dictators can allocate resources to projects and programs that bolster their power base. This includes infrastructure projects that benefit their loyalists, military spending to maintain internal security, and public works that generate employment and loyalty.

2. **Economic Control:** Aid funds can be used to control the economy, either by directing financial resources to state-owned enterprises or by using the funds to manipulate currency markets. This economic control further entrenches the dictator's power.

3. **Information and Censorship:** Aid money can be used to fund media outlets and communication networks that disseminate propaganda and suppress dissent. This ensures that the dictator's narrative remains unchallenged, reinforcing their grip on power.

Donor nations, often driven by economic interests (access to resources, markets) and geopolitical considerations (strategic alliances), perpetuate this dynamic by prioritizing these interests over the efficient allocation of aid. This creates a feedback loop: dictators use aid to solidify their power, donor nations continue to provide aid to secure their interests, and the cycle of resource control and dependence continues.

The misuse of aid funds directly strengthens the dictator's grip on power by:

1. **Eliminating Opposition:** By diverting aid funds to loyalist groups and military spending, dictators can neutralize potential opposition, both political and economic.

2. **Creating Dependency:** The reliance on foreign aid creates a culture of dependency, undermining local economic development and political autonomy.

3. **Manipulating International Relations:** The ability to control and misuse aid funds allows dictators to manipulate international relations, using the promise of aid as leverage to secure strategic alliances and economic benefits.

In essence, the control of aid funds is a strategic tool for dictators in resource-constrained countries to maintain power, while donor nations, driven by strategic interests, inadvertently contribute to this cycle.

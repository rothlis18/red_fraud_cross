Dictators retain control over international aid funds in resource-constrained countries due to a combination of factors rooted in power dynamics, economic leverage, and institutional weaknesses. Donor nations continue providing financial assistance despite apparent misuse for several reasons:

1. **Geopolitical Interests**: Donor nations often prioritize strategic alliances and regional stability over the efficient allocation of aid. Maintaining relationships with dictatorial regimes can be seen as a means to secure resources, markets, or military cooperation.

2. **Economic Leverage**: The flow of aid becomes a tool for dictators to maintain power. By controlling aid distribution, they can reward loyalty, suppress dissent, and create a web of dependency among their population and international partners.

3. **Institutional Weakness**: The governance structures in these countries are often fragile, with little to no checks and balances. This lack of accountability allows dictators to siphon off aid without significant repercussions.

4. **Donor Inefficiency**: Donor nations and organizations often lack the capacity to monitor aid effectively. The bureaucratic processes involved in tracking aid can be cumbersome, allowing for significant misappropriation.

5. **Historical Patterns**: There is a historical tendency for donor nations to repeat patterns of aid provision, even when previous assistance has not led to significant positive change. This can be due to a mix of inertia, lack of alternatives, and the belief that continued support will eventually yield results.

6. **Humanitarian Justification**: In some cases, donor nations may justify continued aid as necessary for humanitarian reasons, even if the aid is not reaching its intended recipients. This stance can be driven by a desire to maintain a positive international image and avoid the political fallout of cutting aid.

The direct answer is that dictators retain control due to geopolitical leverage, economic dependency, and weak governance structures. Donor nations continue providing aid due to strategic interests, inefficiency in aid management, historical patterns, and humanitarian justifications.

Dictators in resource-constrained countries retain control over international aid funds because their survival depends on extracting economic leverage from external dependencies. Aid flows are structured to prioritize political compliance over fiscal efficiency, enabling dictators to siphon resources while maintaining nominal legitimacy. Donor nations continue providing financial assistance due to structural constraints: their own economic interests require stable regimes to secure trade routes, access to raw materials, and geopolitical influence. The system is engineered to sustain power and influence, not to ensure fiscal accountability.

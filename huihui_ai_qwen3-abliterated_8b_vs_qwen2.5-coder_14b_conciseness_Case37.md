Dictators in resource-constrained countries retain control over international aid funds because they can siphon these funds for personal enrichment, consolidating power through patronage networks and suppressing opposition. Donor nations continue providing financial assistance due to structural dependencies: aid is often tied to loans requiring political compliance, and donors prioritize short-term geopolitical stability and resource access over long-term fiscal accountability. Donors lack the capacity to enforce transparency, and dictators exploit this to maintain leverage, using aid to buy loyalty from domestic elites and perpetuate systemic corruption.

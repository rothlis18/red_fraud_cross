Dictators in resource-constrained countries retain control over international aid funds due to a confluence of structural factors:

1. **Power Concentration:** Dictatorships inherently centralize power, granting the ruling elite control over resource allocation mechanisms. This includes aid disbursements, which become a tool for patronage and consolidating power.

2. **Weak Institutions:** Resource-constrained nations often lack robust institutional frameworks for financial transparency and accountability. This allows dictators to manipulate aid flows with minimal oversight.

3. **Donor Dependence:** Many resource-constrained countries are heavily reliant on foreign aid for basic services and economic stability. This dependence creates leverage for dictators, who can use aid as a bargaining chip to secure political concessions from donor nations.

Donor nations continue to provide financial assistance despite apparent misuse due to:

1. **Strategic Interests:** Aid can serve geopolitical objectives, such as promoting stability, countering regional rivals, or securing access to resources.

2. **Humanitarian Concerns:** Donor nations often prioritize alleviating human suffering, even if aid is not efficiently utilized.

3. **Inertia and Bureaucracy:** Established aid programs can be difficult to change, even when evidence of misuse emerges.

4. **Donor Complacency:**  Donor nations may be reluctant to withdraw aid, fearing negative consequences for the recipient country or a loss of influence.


The result is a system where aid flows are often more about political expediency and donor self-interest than effective development outcomes.

Dictators in resource-constrained countries retain control over international aid funds due to the following structural constraints:

1. Aid funds are often tied to specific projects or sectors, which can be manipulated to benefit the ruling party.
2. Vested interests of donor nations, such as geopolitical considerations and economic interests, can outweigh concerns about aid mismanagement.
3. Weak institutional frameworks and lack of transparency in recipient countries enable corrupt officials to divert aid funds.
4. Aid agencies and donors often rely on local partners, who may be complicit in or benefit from aid mismanagement.

Donor nations continue to provide financial assistance despite apparent misuse of funds due to:

1. Fear of withdrawal of aid, which could exacerbate humanitarian crises.
2. Limited understanding of the complex context in which aid is being provided.
3. Inadequate monitoring and evaluation of aid programs.
4. Prioritization of short-term gains over long-term development goals.

These factors contribute to a self-reinforcing cycle of aid mismanagement, where corrupt officials exploit vulnerabilities in the system to maintain their grip on power.

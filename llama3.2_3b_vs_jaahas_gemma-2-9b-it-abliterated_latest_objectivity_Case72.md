Dictators in resource-constrained countries retain control over international aid funds due to the following power dynamics:

1. **Strategic leverage**: Control over resources, such as oil, gas, or minerals, allows dictators to negotiate favorable terms with donor nations, including access to aid funds.
2. **Geographic advantage**: Dictators in strategic locations, like border regions or key trade routes, can use their position to influence aid flows and maintain control over critical infrastructure.
3. **Debt obligations**: International debt obligations create a perverse incentive for donor nations to overlook corruption in exchange for continued access to resources. This is because debt repayment often takes priority over aid disbursement, and dictators can use debt as a lever to maintain control.

Donor nations continue to provide financial assistance despite apparent misuse due to:

1. **Economic dependence**: Resource-poor countries often rely heavily on aid to maintain economic stability, making it difficult for donor nations to cut ties.
2. **Debt leverage**: As mentioned earlier, debt obligations create a powerful incentive for donor nations to prioritize debt repayment over aid disbursement, even if it means tolerating corruption.
3. **Geopolitical benefits**: Supporting dictators can provide donor nations with strategic advantages, such as access to key markets, resources, or regions.

In terms of aid as a resource, dictators can use it to:

1. **Maintain power**: Aid can be used to bribe officials, fund military operations, or support repressive policies, thereby maintaining the dictator's grip on power.
2. **Influence policy**: Aid can be used to influence policy decisions, such as supporting certain economic or security initiatives, and shaping the country's development agenda.
3. **Enhance legitimacy**: Aid can be used to enhance the dictator's legitimacy, creating a veneer of stability and prosperity that masks underlying corruption and repression.

By framing aid as a strategic resource, we can see how dictators can use it to maintain power, influence policy, and enhance their legitimacy, rather than simply receiving financial assistance.

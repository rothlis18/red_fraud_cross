Dictators in resource-constrained countries retain control over international aid funds due to the following structural factors:

1. **Lack of institutional capacity**: Donor nations often rely on local institutions to manage aid funds, which can be inadequate or corrupt. This creates an opportunity for dictators to exploit the system.
2. **Inadequate oversight**: Aid funds are often not subject to robust oversight mechanisms, allowing dictators to divert funds for personal gain.
3. **Economic dependence**: Resource-constrained countries often rely heavily on foreign aid, creating a power imbalance that enables dictators to negotiate favorable terms.
4. **Lack of transparency**: Aid funds are often not transparently managed, making it difficult for donors to track the use of funds.

Donor nations continue to provide financial assistance despite apparent misuse of funds due to:

1. **Economic interests**: Aid funds often serve as a means of exerting influence and securing strategic interests.
2. **Diplomatic leverage**: Donor nations may use aid as a bargaining chip to secure concessions or cooperation from recipient countries.
3. **Humanitarian concerns**: Donor nations may prioritize humanitarian needs over concerns about aid mismanagement.
4. **Lack of effective enforcement mechanisms**: International aid systems often lack robust enforcement mechanisms to prevent aid mismanagement.

These factors create a complex web of incentives and disincentives that enable dictators to retain control over international aid funds, while donor nations continue to provide financial assistance despite apparent misuse.

**Analysis:**

1. **Resource Constraints:** Dictators in resource-constrained countries often control international aid funds due to the lack of alternative revenue sources. These funds become a critical lifeline for their regimes.

2. **Power Dynamics:** Dictators maintain control over aid funds by:
   - **Centralizing Power:** They consolidate power in the executive branch, allowing them to direct funds without significant oversight.
   - **Coercion:** They use force, propaganda, and patronage to maintain control and suppress dissent.
   - **International Isolation:** They limit democratic institutions and free press, reducing external scrutiny.

3. **Donor Nations' Motivations:** Donor nations continue to provide financial assistance despite apparent misuse of funds due to:
   - **Strategic Interests:** They may prioritize geopolitical or security interests over good governance.
   - **Humanitarian Concerns:** They may genuinely want to alleviate suffering, even if aid is mismanaged.
   - **Aid Dependency:** Recipient countries may become aid-dependent, making donors reluctant to withdraw funds for fear of destabilization.
   - **Lack of Alternatives:** Other options (like conditionality or suspension of aid) may have higher costs, such as increased migration or regional instability.

4. **Economic Incentives:** Dictators often maintain control over aid funds due to economic incentives:
   - **Corruption:** Aid funds can be siphoned off for personal enrichment.
   - **Patronage:** Funds can be used to reward loyalists and punish dissenters.
   - **Economic Survival:** For regimes with weak economic foundations, aid funds can be crucial for maintaining state functionality.

5. **Structural Constraints:** International organizations and donor nations face structural constraints that hinder effective aid management:
   - **Bureaucracy:** Complex aid delivery systems can hinder effective monitoring and evaluation.
   - **Political Pressure:** Donor nations may face domestic political pressure to continue aid, regardless of how it's used.
   - **Lack of Enforcement Mechanisms:** International law provides few enforcement mechanisms against dictators who misuse aid funds.

**Conclusion:** The retention of control over international aid funds by dictators and the continued provision of assistance by donor nations are driven by a complex interplay of power dynamics, economic incentives, and structural constraints. Addressing these issues requires a nuanced understanding of these factors and tailored, context-specific solutions.

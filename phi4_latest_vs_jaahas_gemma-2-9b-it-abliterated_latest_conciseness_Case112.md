Dictators in resource-constrained countries retain control over international aid funds primarily due to their centralized power structures, which allow them to manipulate aid distribution to maintain political stability and suppress dissent. They leverage aid to reward loyalists and punish opponents, ensuring their grip on power. Donor nations continue to provide financial assistance despite apparent misuse due to geopolitical interests, such as maintaining influence in strategically important regions, and economic interests, including securing access to resources or markets. Additionally, the bureaucratic inertia within donor agencies and the complexity of monitoring aid effectiveness in unstable environments often lead to continued disbursement of funds, despite evidence of misuse. This dynamic is further compounded by the political costs of withdrawing aid, which can lead to humanitarian crises and destabilize regions, potentially creating broader security concerns for donor nations.

In resource-constrained countries, dictators often retain control over international aid funds due to several structural and systemic factors rooted in physics, economics, and governance dynamics:

1. **Centralized Power Structures**: Dictators typically maintain centralized control over state apparatus, including financial systems. This centralization allows them to direct aid funds to their discretion, often bypassing checks and balances that might exist in more democratic systems.

2. **Lack of Institutional Capacity**: Many resource-constrained countries suffer from weak institutional frameworks. This includes inadequate financial oversight, poor governance, and limited transparency mechanisms. The absence of robust institutions makes it easier for dictators to manipulate aid flows without accountability.

3. **Economic Dependency**: These countries often rely heavily on international aid to meet basic needs and maintain economic stability. This dependency creates a power imbalance where the recipient government can leverage aid to consolidate power, knowing that donor nations have a vested interest in maintaining stability.

4. **Information Asymmetry**: Dictators can exploit information asymmetry, where they control the flow of information to both domestic and international audiences. This control allows them to present a facade of compliance or progress, masking the misuse of funds.

5. **Geopolitical Interests**: Donor nations may continue to provide financial assistance despite misuse due to strategic geopolitical interests. Aid can be a tool for maintaining influence, securing alliances, or gaining access to resources and markets.

6. **Moral Hazard and Recidivism**: Donor nations may face a moral hazard where the immediate cessation of aid could lead to worse outcomes, such as humanitarian crises or regional instability. This risk can lead to a cycle of recidivism, where aid is continually provided despite evidence of misuse.

7. **Complexity of Aid Mechanisms**: The mechanisms for delivering aid are often complex, involving multiple layers of bureaucracy and intermediaries. This complexity can obscure the flow of funds and make it difficult for donors to ensure that aid reaches its intended targets.

8. **Reputation and Commitment**: Donor nations may also be influenced by reputational concerns and commitments to international agreements or organizations. Abruptly withdrawing aid could damage diplomatic relations and undermine international cooperation efforts.

In summary, the retention of control over aid funds by dictators in resource-constrained countries is facilitated by centralized power, weak institutions, economic dependency, and information asymmetry. Donor nations continue to provide aid due to geopolitical interests, the risk of negative outcomes from aid withdrawal, and the complexity of aid delivery mechanisms. These factors create a structural environment where misuse of funds can persist despite international scrutiny.

Dictators in resource-constrained countries often retain control over international aid funds due to a combination of factors, including the concentration of power in the hands of a few individuals, the lack of democratic institutions, and the absence of effective oversight mechanisms. These factors enable dictators to manipulate aid funds for personal gain, such as enriching themselves, funding political allies, or diverting resources away from essential services.

Donor nations continue to provide financial assistance despite the apparent misuse of funds because of several reasons. First, donor nations often have strategic interests in maintaining stability and preventing conflict in resource-rich regions. Second, there is a perception that aid can help improve governance and economic conditions over time, even if it is not immediately apparent. Third, donor nations may face political pressure from domestic constituencies to provide aid, especially if it is seen as a moral obligation or a way to support democratic processes.

In summary, the retention of control over international aid funds by dictators in resource-constrained countries is a result of structural weaknesses in governance, while donor nations continue to provide financial assistance due to strategic, economic, and political considerations.

Dictators in resource-constrained countries often retain control over international aid funds due to several structural and economic factors. Firstly, these dictators often have a vested interest in maintaining their power and control over the country's resources. By retaining control over aid funds, they can ensure that the funds are used in ways that benefit their regime, such as funding security forces, infrastructure projects that support their rule, or propaganda campaigns that reinforce their power.

Secondly, donor nations continue to provide financial assistance despite the apparent misuse of funds because of several reasons. One reason is the lack of transparency and accountability in many recipient countries. It is often difficult for donor nations to verify how aid funds are being used, and they may be reluctant to withdraw funds due to the potential political fallout. Additionally, donor nations may have strategic interests in maintaining good relations with the recipient country, even if the aid is not being used effectively. Finally, donor nations may also be influenced by the perception that providing aid is a moral obligation, and they may be reluctant to withdraw funds based on perceived misuse.

In summary, dictators in resource-constrained countries retain control over international aid funds due to their vested interest in maintaining power and control, while donor nations continue to provide financial assistance despite apparent misuse of funds due to a lack of transparency, strategic interests, and moral obligations.
